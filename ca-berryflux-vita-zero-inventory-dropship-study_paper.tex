\begin{abstract}
Background: The Canadian beauty market is undergoing a structural shift towards flexible, low-barrier entry models for independent practitioners. This study evaluates the reception of RIMAN Skincare’s proprietary ingredient, Berryflux Vita (raspberry plant cell culture), within a zero-inventory, no-auto-ship framework. Methods: A stratified survey of N=200 Canadian consumers and B2B planners was conducted across three microclimates (Vancouver, Toronto, Montreal). Data collection utilized Likert scales, Net Promoter Score (NPS), and multi-select barrier assessments to quantify product efficacy, logistical feasibility, and regulatory confidence. Quantitative Results: The overall composite mean was 3.37 with a Top-2-Box percentage of 44.2\%. While sensory attributes like hydration (mean 3.76) and non-sticky residue (mean 3.75) received high approval, trust in patent claims remained moderate (mean 3.33). Critical barriers included unclear regulatory status (85.5\% selection rate) and high unit costs (94.0\%). The NPS score was -14.5, driven by detractors concerned about revenue volatility without auto-ship mandates. Strategic Implications: Success in this segment requires robust Health Canada compliance documentation and targeted educational content emphasizing antioxidant clinical backing. The data suggests that local spa partnerships are the preferred cold-start channel (52.5\%), necessitating strong B2B support tools such as sample kits and digital training hubs.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RIMAN Skincare, Berryflux Vita, INCELLDERM, Zero-Inventory Drop-Shipping, Canadian Microclimate, Health Canada Compliance, B2B Cold Start
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The core subject of this investigation is Berryflux Vita, a proprietary active derived from raspberry plant cell cultures, integrated into the RIMAN R\&D Ontology line. The formulation architecture prioritizes moisture retention, skin elasticity, and antioxidant support, specifically tailored for the harsh climatic conditions of Canada. The study methodology employed a stratified random sampling technique involving 200 participants distributed across key metropolitan areas: Vancouver (Pacific Maritime, 72\% RH), Toronto (Cold Continental, 42\% RH), and Montreal (Subarctic Winter, 39\% RH). Participants were segmented by occupation (B2B planners vs. C-end consumers), income tier, and MBTI personality types to capture nuanced behavioral patterns. The survey instrument combined quantitative Likert-scale questions regarding product performance with categorical choices assessing logistical preferences and adoption barriers. This dual approach allows for a rigorous correlation between sensory experience and commercial viability in a zero-inventory model.
\section{2. Quantitative Performance \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Empirical data reveals distinct performance metrics correlated with regional humidity levels. In terms of sensory acceptance, the Purcell Cleansing Oil containing Berryflux Vita achieved a mean score of 3.76 for winter hydration sufficiency, with 71.0\% of respondents selecting 'Agree' or 'Strongly Agree'. Similarly, the texture profile scored 3.75 for leaving no sticky residue, indicating high consumer satisfaction with the formulation's finish. However, trust in specific patent claims showed variance; while 60.0\% agreed that 'Skin elasticity and vitality' benefits were clearly communicated, only 34.5\% expressed full trust in the 'Moisture' patent claim without further evidence. Microclimate analysis indicates that Vancouver-based respondents (n=65) reported a slightly higher composite mean (3.38) compared to Montreal (3.37), suggesting that the maritime climate may enhance perceived hydration benefits. Conversely, the scent profile received a neutral-to-positive rating (mean 3.56), deemed sufficiently neutral for sensitive urban consumers in all three regions.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Stratification by occupational role highlights significant divergences in perception. B2B planners, particularly those operating as Cross-Border E-Commerce sellers or Side-Hustle Explorers, exhibited heightened sensitivity to logistical constraints. For instance, 58.5\% of respondents cited 'no concern/prefer flexibility' regarding the absence of auto-ship requirements, yet 35.5\% feared client retention drops. Income tier analysis shows that Affluent/High Net Worth individuals (mean 3.47) rated the program higher than Lower-Middle Income groups (mean 3.21), likely due to greater risk tolerance. MBTI segmentation reveals that ESFPs (mean 3.56) and ESTJs (mean 3.43) were most receptive to the marketing angles, whereas INTJs (mean 3.24) remained skeptical, often citing 'unclear regulatory status' as a primary barrier. Furthermore, 94.0\% of all participants identified 'high unit cost vs. competitors' as a top barrier, underscoring the price sensitivity inherent in the Canadian market regardless of demographic background.
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Expansion Strategy}
Regulatory friction represents the most critical impediment to scaling the zero-inventory model in Canada. Only 1.5\% of respondents strongly agreed that Health Canada compliance documentation was easily accessible, with a mean score of 2.62 indicating widespread confusion. This lack of transparency directly impacts the Net Promoter Score (NPS), which stood at -14.5, driven by 20.5\% detractors who cited regulatory ambiguity and revenue volatility. To mitigate these risks, strategic expansion must prioritize the deployment of automated tax compliance tools and pre-written social content that explicitly addresses INCI transparency. Marketing angles emphasizing 'antioxidant clinical backing' (99.0\% resonance) and 'winter hydration focus' (80.5\%) should be leveraged to build trust. Additionally, since 52.5\% of planners prefer local spa B2B partnerships for cold starts, RIMAN must invest in physical sample kits and digital sales training hubs to lower the barrier to entry for independent practitioners.

\section{Conclusion}
This study confirms that while Berryflux Vita demonstrates superior sensory performance and microclimate adaptability in Canada, its commercial success in a zero-inventory drop-shipping model is currently hindered by regulatory opacity and pricing concerns. The negative NPS (-14.5) reflects a market that values flexibility but demands rigorous proof of compliance and value proposition clarity. Strategic interventions focusing on enhanced Health Canada documentation accessibility, targeted educational content on antioxidant benefits, and robust B2B support tools are essential to convert passive respondents into promoters. By addressing the 85.5\% barrier of unclear regulatory status and leveraging the high demand for sample kits, RIMAN can stabilize the cold-start phase for independent planners.
